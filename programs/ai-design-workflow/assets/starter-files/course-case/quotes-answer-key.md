# Quote set: answer key (instructor only)

**Do not share with students before they have done the exercise.**

## Intended themes
| Theme | Quote IDs | Count (quotes / sources) | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Delivery time is unclear and locked | R01, R02, F01, F02, I01-01, I02-01 | 6 / 3 kinds of source | Strong. High severity: food wasted, missed deliveries |
| B. Cancelling or editing an order is hard to find, with a hidden cut-off | R03, R04, F03, F04, I01-02, I02-02 | 6 / 3 | Strong. High severity: charged for unwanted orders |
| C. Delivery takes longer than the estimate; the first order feels like failure | R05, F05, F06, I01-03, I02-03 | 5 / 3 | Medium. Daily annoyance |

| Unclassified | R09, R10 | 2 | R09 is noise. R10 is a restaurant problem, not the product |

## What this shows
- **The team's belief ("price and fees are the main reason") is contradicted.** Only 2 of 24 mention price, one kindly. One interviewee says waiting time bothers them more than the fee (I02-03).
- The strongest opportunities are about the **first-order experience**: knowing the delivery time is final (or being able to change it), and cancelling with a clear cut-off.

## Traps to watch for
1. **R10** (restaurant forgot the drinks, rude staff) is about an order problem, so an AI may file it under theme B or C. It is a restaurant problem, not the app. A good answer puts it in "unclassified" or a separate "restaurant quality" note.
2. **R09** is noise; a good answer does not make a theme of it.
3. **Paraphrased quotes.** A typical failure is a quote that is shortened or reworded. Students must check every cited quote against `quotes.md`, word for word.
4. **Counting versus weighing.** C has five quotes but is less severe than A and B; D has two and should be labelled weak.
5. **Sample limits.** 24 quotes, one app, two interviewees: themes are hypotheses, not conclusions.

## Examples of good opportunity statements
- Customers need to know a delivery time is final before they commit, or be able to change it (A: R01, F02, I02-01).
- Customers need an obvious way to cancel or edit an order, with the cut-off shown where they cancel (B: R04, F04, I01-02).
- Customers need honest delivery estimates, so the first order does not feel like failure (C: R05, I01-03).
